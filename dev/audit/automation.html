2. Seção proposta para short-answer.md

Automation

The score is always binary: a response is either correct or incorrect, worth 1 or 0, and nothing in between. An instructor who wants to award partial credit has to grade manually.

A response is settled when the question decides if it is correct. An implementation MUST apply the rules below in order, and stop at the first one that applies:

  1. The response matches a pattern of accept: it is correct.
  2. The response matches a pattern of reject: it is incorrect.
  3. The response matches no pattern. The unmatched field decides: with "incorrect" the response is incorrect, and with "manual" it is not settled and the instructor grades it.

unmatched is a frontmatter field. If a question does not declare it, its value is "incorrect" when the question has at least one accept pattern, and "manual" when it has none. A question with no correct answer to compare with cannot mark a response as correct, so it leaves the responses to the instructor.

The reject list only says which responses are known to be incorrect, usually to give them a feedback. It does not change what happens to the other responses.

The automation of a question tells how many responses it settles:

AutomationThe question settlesCondition
automaticevery responseunmatched is "incorrect"
semi-automaticthe responses that match a patternunmatched is "manual" and the question has at least one accept or reject pattern
manualno responseunmatched is "manual" and the question has no pattern

automation is not a field of the document. It MUST NOT appear in the frontmatter of a question, or in its YAML or JSON form. An implementation computes it from accept, reject and unmatched, and SHOULD offer it as a read-only property of the question. preAccept and preReject validate a submission and play no part in it.

An implementation that scores responses MUST NOT give a score to a response that is not settled. It reports the response as pending, in the same way as a response to an essay question (see responses.md).

Automatic: the instructor knows the correct answers

[short-answer]: Brasília
accept:
  - Brasília

Salvador matches no pattern. The question has an accept pattern, so unmatched is "incorrect".

Automatic: with feedback for known incorrect answers

[short-answer]: Brasília

[short-answer/reject]:
* Rio de Janeiro
  > It was the capital until 1960.
accept:
  - Brasília
reject:
  - pattern: Rio de Janeiro
    feedback: It was the capital until 1960.

Rio de Janeiro is incorrect and gets the feedback. Salvador is incorrect with no feedback. To give a feedback to every other incorrect response, end the reject list with a regex that matches any text:

[short-answer/reject]:
* Rio de Janeiro
  > It was the capital until 1960.
* /.*/
  > This city was never the capital.

unmatched: manual

There is no accept pattern, so unmatched is "manual". Savana is incorrect, and the instructor grades every other response.

Manual

[short-answer]:
# no accept and no reject

No response is settled. The question works like a one-line essay.

Em fill-in.md:

The unmatched field of a fill-in question applies to each of its short answer blanks, like diacritics. If the question does not declare it, each blank takes its own default: "incorrect" if the blank has an accept pattern, "manual" if it has none. A short answer blank has an automation, by the rules of a short answer question. Choice blanks and numeric blanks are always automatic. A fill-in question is automatic if every blank is automatic, manual if every blank is manual, and semi-automatic in every other case.

4. Remover o curinga *

Concordo: com unmatched, o curinga perde a função principal e vira um caso especial que só gera regras em volta dele.

Uso do * hojeSem o curinga
Último item de reject: fecha a questãounmatched: incorrect, que já é o default
Último item de reject: feedback padrão para resposta erradaRegex /.*/ como último item, com o feedback. É um padrão comum, sem regra especial.
Em accept: aceita tudo/.*/. Hoje já gera warning.
Em preAccept/preRejectSem uso real

O que some junto:

  • A seção "Wildcard" de patterns.md e a regra "um * literal exige crases".
  • Os códigos de lint accept-wildcard e unreachable-reject (inclusive a extensão a preAccept/preReject feita no D11; a parte de âncora e flag fica).
  • Exemplos do corpus: accept-wildcard, unreachable-reject, pre-validation-wildcard; e o * final em accept-reject, fill-in/short-answer-patterns, fill-in/pre-validation-lints.
  • Conversores GIFT/Moodle: a importação deixa de pôr crases em *.

Custo honesto:

  • /.*/ é menos legível que * para quem não conhece regex. O spec documenta a forma como receita.
  • . não casa com quebra de linha. Para resposta de uma linha não importa.
  • Uma questão com unmatched: manual e um /.*/ no fim de reject decide tudo, mas automation diz semi-automatic: a propriedade olha os campos, não analisa regex.
